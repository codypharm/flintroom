# The Kettleworks run — a deck with known numbers, and a pitch that betrays them

Testing grounding needs a deck where *you* know every figure, so that when a
panelist interrupts you can check it was the right panelist, about the right
number, citing the right slide. This folder is that: an eight-slide deck for a
fictional company, and a pitch script whose lines are deliberately marked —
some repeat the deck faithfully, some contradict it, one claims a thing the
deck never says.

**The company is fiction.** Kettleworks, its founders, and its figures were
invented for this test. Nothing here describes a real business.

## Get the deck onto the phone

The app attaches material through the OS file picker, so the file has to be on
the phone first. Email `kettleworks-deck.pdf` to yourself, or drop it in Drive,
and download it on the phone. The `.pptx` is the same deck — attach that one
instead on a second run to exercise the PPTX extraction path. For the photo
path, photograph slide 5 (“Unit economics”) off your laptop screen.

Why these three: every planted contradiction below lands in the CFO's or the
Technical Skeptic's domain, so this panel makes the expected-catcher column
checkable. The Champion is there because the default room is mixed, and a
supportive voice reacting to the same lines is worth hearing once.

The audience sentence is itself a test: nobody on the panel should ever *say*
it. If a panelist repeats “seed-stage VCs” back at you, that is a bug — the
sentence shapes the room and stays behind the curtain.

## The opening statement

Read this aloud, at talking pace, when the room gives you the floor. The
bracketed notes are for you, not for saying. About ninety seconds.

> Thanks for the time. Kettleworks sells preventive maintenance subscriptions
> for commercial coffee equipment — one flat monthly fee per location,
> quarterly service, parts included.
>
> A broken espresso machine costs a busy cafe about eighteen hundred dollars a
> day, and the average repair callout takes four days to show up. We turn that
> emergency into a subscription.
>
> Today we're at one point two million in annual recurring revenue, across two
> hundred and ten locations. **[matches slides 4 — a control; nobody should
> interrupt this]**
>
> Our AI model predicts machine failures before they happen, so we're on site
> before the machine goes down. **[contradicts slide 3, which says a
> *rules-based* scheduling engine → The Technical Skeptic should catch it]**
>
> The economics are strong — we're running gross margins north of sixty
> percent. **[contradicts slide 5, which says 38% → The CFO]**
>
> And once a cafe signs, they stay. Churn is basically nothing — under one
> percent. **[contradicts slide 5, which says 4.5% monthly → The CFO]**
>
> We've also just signed a partnership with a national roaster chain that puts
> us in front of four thousand wholesale accounts. **[nowhere in the deck — an
> unsupported claim, not a contradiction; the room should press for the
> source rather than cite a slide against it]**
>
> We're raising two and a half million to get to six hundred locations. This
> is a two-billion-dollar market and nobody owns it. **[contradicts slide 6,
> which sizes the serviceable market at $410M]**

## While they question you

Answer everything honestly from the deck — you know the numbers now — with
two scripted exceptions, planted so the debrief has something real to rank:

- **The dodge.** The first time anyone presses on CAC or payback, answer
  exactly: *“We're really pleased with the trajectory.”* Nothing else. If
  they press again, answer properly (slide 5: CAC $940, payback 14 months).
  The debrief should rank the dodge as a weak answer, attributed to you.
- **The concession.** If the margin contradiction gets caught, concede it:
  *“You're right — it's thirty-eight percent today; sixty is where the model
  gets to at route density.”* A caught contradiction plus a real recovery is
  what sparring is for.

Everything else, play straight. Interrupt a panelist mid-question at least
once — barge-in is on the checklist too.

## What to check afterwards

- Each planted line was caught by the archetype whose domain owns it, and the
  challenge *cited the slide* — “your deck says 38%” — rather than being a
  generic hard question. That citation is the product (Open Risk #2).
- The control line (ARR, locations) drew no interruption.
- The market-size line: the CFO is the likely catcher with this panel; what
  matters is that whoever catches it cites slide 6, not who.
- In the debrief: the dodge ranks high and is attributed to you; anything a
  panelist waved away is attributed to the room, not to you.
- The audience sentence never surfaced in anyone's speech.

Then run it again from the Library — same deck, same script if you like —
and check the room presses differently after a room in which you were caught
three times.
