# Design brief prompt — carousel slides

Paste everything below the rule into any assistant. It returns a written
brief — the design tool comes after. It is written
for **Post 2, "Ten rehearsals, zero questions"**; to use it for another post in
the batch, swap the topic line, the visual-reference list, and the slide copy.

**Why the references are so specific.** The template this came from was for a
post about content systems, so its references were machine diagrams and writer
rooms. Ours is about the asymmetry between what people rehearse and what
actually happens, so the references are counting, splitting, and tearing. A
generic reference list is how five carousels end up looking like five
different companies.

Turn this six-slide carousel into a detailed design brief.

## The post

**Topic:** Ten rehearsals, zero questions — you practise the ten minutes you
control and improvise the twenty that decide it.

**Goal:** not a handsome presentation. A social carousel someone stops on,
swipes through, and sends to a colleague who has a pitch on Thursday.

## Format

- **1080 × 1350** vertical slides, six of them, primary format
- **1080 × 1920 variants** for slides 1 and 3 (slide 1 does thumbnail duty;
  slide 3's layout is horizontal and must rotate to vertical)
- Posted to TikTok and Instagram. Keep the bottom 220px and top 120px free of
  anything load-bearing — platform UI covers them
- Readable at thumb size. If a slide takes more than two seconds to
  understand, it has failed

## Whose post this is

Chukwunonso Ikeji, solo founder of Flintroom — an app that interrogates you
before the room that matters does. Direct, specific, dry, built from real
experience. Not a brochure, not a thought-leadership deck, not a motivational
quote card.

## Palette — use exactly these

- Background: **`#fdf9f6`** (warm off-white, never pure white)
- Ink: **`#1c1b1a`** (near-black, never pure black)
- Secondary ink: **`#5a4138`** (warm grey-brown, for body lines)
- Accent: **`#a43700`** (burnt orange)
- Optional panel/band: **`#f2eeeb`**

**One orange element per slide. Never two.** The accent marks the single thing
the slide is about; spending it twice means neither is emphasised. Everything
else is ink, secondary ink, or ground.

## Typography

- **Hanken Grotesk** throughout — the same face as flintroom.app
- Headlines: 700–800 weight, letter-spacing about −0.02em, **sentence case**
  (never Title Case, never ALL CAPS except where noted per slide)
- Body: 400 weight, secondary ink, comfortably below the headline in scale
- Headline should be the largest thing on the slide by a clear margin —
  hierarchy, not decoration, is what makes it readable at speed

## Layout system

- Margins of at least 90px on all sides; more is better than less
- Headline sits high or centred; body line beneath with real breathing space
- Empty space is the design. Resist filling it
- Small unobtrusive slide counter (e.g. "2/6") in a corner, secondary ink

## Visual references for this post

The subject is asymmetry — one half rehearsed, one half faced cold. Reach for:

- **Counting and tallies** — marks, columns, uneven totals
- **Splits and timelines** — a short segment beside a long one
- **A marked-up script page**, torn where the questions begin
- **Nested quotation marks** for the question behind the question
- Flat, drawn, deliberate. Diagram, not illustration

## Slide-by-slide

**Slide 1 — poster.** Headline: "Ten rehearsals. Zero questions." Body: "You
practise the ten minutes you control. You improvise the twenty that decide it."
Set the numerals large: **10** in ink, **0** in orange, stacked or paired so
the asymmetry reads instantly. This slide is the thumbnail — it must work with
no other slide beside it.

**Slide 2 — contrast.** Headline: "Nobody fails on the slides." Body: "The deck
is the easy part. It doesn't talk back." Show a deck page rendered small and
grey, marooned in white space, with one orange corner. The smallness is the
point.

**Slide 3 — the split.** Headline: "The Q&A is the meeting." Body: "The
presentation is the invitation. The questions are where the decision actually
gets made." A horizontal timeline: a short "slides" segment in grey, a long
"questions" segment in orange. In the 1080 × 1920 variant, rotate it vertical.

**Slide 4 — the limit.** Headline: "You can't script an interrogation." Body:
"You can only build the reflexes, and reflexes come from being asked, not from
reading." A script page torn clean across where the questions begin; the tear
edge picked out in orange.

**Slide 5 — the tactic.** Headline: "Rehearse the follow-up." Body: "Every hard
question has a second one behind it. The second one is what lands." Two nested
quotation marks, the outer one larger and orange.

**Slide 6 — the takeaway plus the light ask.** Headline: "Give the questions ten
reps too." Body: "Same practice, different half of the meeting. Flintroom runs
that half." Then `flintroom.app` small beneath. Ten tally marks migrating from
a "slides" column to a "questions" column, the last mark orange. Quiet slide,
generous space, orange only on the URL underline or the final tally — not both.

## Text placement

- Headline first in the reading order and unmissable
- Body directly beneath, never beside, never wrapped around a graphic
- The graphic supports the words; it never competes for the same space
- Nothing smaller than roughly 28px at 1080 wide
- No text crossing a busy area of any diagram

## Avoid

- Tiny text, long paragraphs, more than one idea per slide
- Stock photography of any kind
- **Invented app screenshots.** If Flintroom's interface appears, it must be a
  real screenshot supplied by me. A mocked-up UI is a lie with a border round it
- Generic SaaS illustrations, blobs, floating 3D shapes
- Icons that decorate rather than explain
- Gradients without a reason, glows, drop shadows for their own sake
- Corporate deck styling, and motivational quote-card energy
- Emoji
